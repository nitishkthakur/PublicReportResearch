CEO Commentary

Citigroup CEO Jane Fraser highlighted a strong quarter with net income of $4.1 billion, driven by growth in all five interconnected businesses, improved returns, and positive operating leverage. She emphasized progress in Services, Markets, Banking, and Wealth segments. Key points included:

  • Services recorded its best first-quarter revenue in a decade.Markets revenue increased 12% due to strong client activity and monetization.
  • Banking revenue rose 12% with M&A revenue nearly doubling YoY.
  • Wealth revenues surged 24% across all client segments.
  • USPB revenue growth of 2%, driven by Branded Cards.
  • Return of $2.8 billion in capital to shareholders, including $1.75 billion in buybacks as part of a $20 billion plan.

Positives and Negatives

Positives

  • Revenue Growth: Revenues increased 3% YoY, driven by all five business segments. Markets (12%), Wealth (24%), and Banking (12%) showed strong growth.Improved Returns: ROCE (8.0%) and RoTCE (9.1%) rose YoY, indicating better equity performance.
  • Capital Returns: $2.8 billion returned to shareholders, with a 74% payout ratio, reflecting disciplined capital management.
  • Book Value Growth: Book value per share rose 5% YoY to $103.90, and tangible book value increased 6% to $91.52.
  • Wealth Growth: Wealth revenues up 24% YoY, driven by higher investment fee revenues and client investment assets up 16%.

Negatives

  • Decline in "All Other" Segment: Managed basis revenues fell 39% YoY due to divestitures (Asia, Mexico) and Mexican peso depreciation. Net loss worsened to $(870M) from $(477M) YoY.
  • Higher Cost of Credit: Total cost of credit rose 15% to $2.7B, fueled by macroeconomic deterioration and credit losses in USPB's card portfolios. < Operating Expenses: While overall operating expenses fell 5% (to $13.4B), certain segments like Markets saw a 2% increase, and USPB faced higher legal and advertising costs.
  • Volatility in Specific Segments: Non-interest revenue in Services declined 4% due to Securities Services' loss of episodic fees and FX impacts. USPB's Retail Services revenue dropped 11% from higher partner payment accruals.
  • CET1 Capital Ratio Decline: CET1 ratio fell to 13.4% from 13.6% in 4Q'24, impacted by share repurchases and higher risk-weighted assets.
